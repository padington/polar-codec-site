Can polar coordinates make round videos smaller?

What we measured on real Telegram circles, and what the literature says.

A Telegram video circle is an ordinary square video file with a round mask drawn on top, so the codec already spends bytes on corners nobody sees. We asked whether a codec would do better if it saw the disc as a disc: we unrolled it into a polar strip (angle along one axis, radius along the other), compressed that strip with a standard, unmodified video codec, and rolled it back.

On 30 real clips the plain square file was smaller every single time. At the same picture quality the polar version needed about 1.8× the bytes, and even the gentlest disc-to-square map cost about 1.4×. The loss is mostly in motion prediction, not in the resampling: a head that turns or nods becomes a warp in the strip that the codec cannot track with its usual "shift the block" moves.

The one place polar maths could still help is as a motion model for rotation and zoom, and AV1 already provides such a model on the plain square. So we re-ran the same clips under AV1: the square's lead shrinks a little (polar 1.76× instead of 1.82×, the gentlest map 1.19× instead of 1.37×), and polar's only win, a fast spin, survives AV1's own rotation tools (−34 % under SVT-AV1; −21 % with libaom's warps on against −32 % with them off). AV1 closes part of the gap on the square, but at fast spin polar still wins and at realistic motion it still loses. Verdict unchanged: drop the polar file format.
