Annotated bibliography for the polar-codec project. Every entry below was checked against the original source (full text, abstract page, or publisher/index record) on 2026-09-06. Where only a snippet or index record could be reached, the entry says so. Numbers that could not be confirmed were removed.

Words used below. Bitrate = bytes per second of video. PSNR = a "how close to the original" score in dB; +1 dB is a clearly visible improvement. BD-rate = the average bitrate saving at equal quality (negative = fewer bytes). QP = the quantiser setting of a video codec; higher QP = fewer bytes, lower quality. ROI = region of interest, a part of the picture the encoder is told to treat as more important. Foveation = spending detail only where the viewer's eye is pointed, because the eye cannot resolve the periphery. Block codec = H.264 / HEVC / AV1 style coder that works on square pixel blocks.

Project numbers referenced in the takeaways (30 real Telegram circles, x264, equal disc quality, plain square = 1.00): elliptical disc-to-square 1.37, Shirley-Chiu 1.62, polar equal-area 1.76, polar strip 1.82, polar rim-Nyquist 2.03; resampling alone costs ~12 points, the 1.6x pixel budget alone +32 %; polar loss sits at the rim (-3.0 dB rim vs -0.6 dB centre); polar only wins on a synthetic 3 deg/frame spin (-14 %).

Lens 1. Polar / log-polar / space-variant video coding and non-translational motion models

Weiman 1990 - the origin of "log-polar video compression"

C. F. R. Weiman, "Video compression via log polar mapping," Proc. SPIE 1295, Real-Time Image Processing II, 1990, doi:10.1117/12.21244. Same scheme in US patent 5,103,306 (Weiman & Evans, filed 1990, granted 1992). Links: https://doi.org/10.1117/12.21244 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US5103306A/en Verified: SPIE record + abstract snippet ("compressing real time color imagery by factors in the range of 1600-to-1 ... for remote driving ... match the resolution gradient of human vision"); patent full text read. - What they did: three-stage pipeline for remote driving: (1) log-polar resampling matched to the fall-off of human acuity ("approximately 25:1"), (2) split into low-res colour and 1-bit edge channels ("approximately 8:1"), (3) ordinary DCT/DPCM ("further 8:1"); product "approximately 1600:1". Ratios are design targets; no equal-quality PSNR comparison is reported. - For round selfies: the 25:1 polar stage throws away rim resolution on the assumption the operator looks at the centre. A circle message needs uniform quality across the disc (hair, chin, hands sit at the rim), so that stage is unavailable and only the resampling cost remains. This is the root of the "polar compresses" folklore.

Geisler & Perry 1998 - real-time foveated video stays Cartesian

W. S. Geisler, J. S. Perry, "Real-time foveated multiresolution system for low-bandwidth video communication," Proc. SPIE 3299, Human Vision and Electronic Imaging III, pp. 294-305, 1998, doi:10.1117/12.320120. Links: https://doi.org/10.1117/12.320120 ; free PDF https://svi.cps.utexas.edu/spie1998.pdf Verified: full text read. - What they did: foveated multiresolution-pyramid coder on a Pentium PC, 5-6 resolution regions around a mouse/eye-tracker fixation point, raised-cosine blending, motion compensation per level, contrast-sensitivity quantisation, zero-tree coding. Strong foveation = 19x fewer pyramid elements, moderate = 5.5x; ~20-25 fps at 256x256. "Claire" talking head: 0.043 bpp unfoveated (36.7 dB) vs 0.020 bpp foveated, and the authors write that foveation "only adds a little to the total compression because the motion is primarily confined to the unfoveated region". "Mall": 0.092 -> 0.013 bpp (7x). They note that earlier real-time foveation systems (incl. Weiman) "used special purpose hardware". - For round selfies: a face-centred clip is exactly where foveation buys least, because the face already carries most of the bits. Expect single-digit savings from any rim-deprioritisation, and get them with a QP map, not a new pixel grid.

Li et al. 2021 - log-polar into a standard codec flickers; a rectilinear-friendly warp is 10 % cheaper

D. Li, R. Du, A. Babu, C. D. Brumar, A. Varshney, "A Log-Rectilinear Transformation for Foveated 360-degree Video Streaming," IEEE TVCG 27(5):2638-2647, 2021 (IEEE VR 2021), doi:10.1109/TVCG.2021.3067762. Links: https://doi.org/10.1109/TVCG.2021.3067762 ; project page https://augmentariumlab.github.io/foveated-360-video/ Verified: abstract via IEEE record, author PDF and project page. - What they did: client-server foveated 360-degree streaming for eye-tracked headsets; compared log-polar subsampling against a log-rectilinear transform with summed-area-table filtering, both fed to off-the-shelf codecs. Abstract: "conventional log-polar foveated rendering suffers from a number of visual artifacts such as aliasing and flickering"; the new transform "heavily reduces flickering compared to log-polar subsampling while also yielding an additional 10% reduction in bandwidth usage" (project page says 11 %). - For round selfies: the closest precedent to our pipeline (polar remap -> x264) and an independent finding that a polar grid is a poor input for block codecs: rim/centre sampling changes create temporal shimmer that costs bits, matching our rim loss and the fact that the inverse warp does not denoise.

Schwarz & Behnke 2025 - modern foveated compression = QP map in an unmodified HEVC

M. Schwarz, S. Behnke, "Foveated Compression for Immersive Telepresence Visualization," IEEE Conference on Telepresence 2025, arXiv:2510.19848. Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.19848 Verified: abstract and full PDF read. - What they did: eye-tracking-driven QP delta map fed to an NVENC HEVC encoder (QP per 32x32 macroblock) in the NimbRo avatar system; "bandwidth can be reduced to a third without sacrificing immersion". They discuss the alternative, "foveated warping" (compress the periphery into less space, code, de-warp, with an axis-aligned variant to fill the codec's rectangular input), and note that "the warping introduces additional sampling effects" and requires de-warping, whereas QP modulation does not. - For round selfies: if the goal is "spend fewer bits on the rim", the proven tool is a QP/quality map (x264 zones/qpfile, AV1 delta-q), keeping the square grid. Even the foveation community treats pixel warping as the inferior option.

Eichenseer et al. 2015 - radial-aware motion search for fisheye: ~1 dB

A. Eichenseer, M. Baetz, J. Seiler, A. Kaup, "A Hybrid Motion Estimation Technique for Fisheye Video Sequences Based on Equisolid Re-Projection," IEEE ICIP 2015, pp. 3565-3569; arXiv:2211.16995. Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16995 Verified: abstract. - What they did: block matching that back-projects into the perspective domain and re-projects with the equisolid fisheye model, chosen per block against ordinary translational matching. Average luma PSNR gain up to 1.14 dB (synthetic), 0.96 dB (real); best 2.40 / 1.39 dB. - For round selfies: fisheye is the industrial case of "round content with radial geometry" and the answer was to change the motion model inside the codec, not to resample into polar; even that gives ~1 dB. A selfie disc is a perspective image with almost no radial distortion, so a radial-aware model would gain less still.

Eichenseer, Baetz & Kaup 2019 - journal version, ~1.5 dB, periphery is the hard part

A. Eichenseer, M. Baetz, A. Kaup, "Motion Estimation for Fisheye Video With an Application to Temporal Resolution Enhancement," IEEE TCSVT 29(8):2376-2390, 2019; arXiv:2303.00433. Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00433 Verified: abstract. - What they did: extended the projection-aware block matching with peripheral handling and calibration data; average gains 1.45 dB (synthetic) / 1.51 dB (real) over conventional block matching; 0.79 / 0.76 dB for frame-rate up-conversion. - For round selfies: in every radial layout the outer ring is where prediction is worst; our guard-row (+11 points) and rim-Nyquist (2.03x) results are consistent. The literature's fix is better prediction in the periphery, not more pixels there.

Eichenseer & Kaup 2014 - warp toward rectilinear before HEVC gains ~1 dB

A. Eichenseer, A. Kaup, "Coding of distortion-corrected fisheye video sequences using H.265/HEVC," IEEE ICIP 2014, pp. 4132-4136; arXiv:2211.16967. Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16967 Verified: abstract. - What they did: HEVC coding of fisheye video with distortion correction as pre-processing (rectify, code) versus post-processing (code fisheye, rectify after). Pre-processing gains: max 1.91 dB intra / 1.37 dB inter; average 1.16 dB intra / 0.95 dB inter. - For round selfies: the mirror image of our experiment. Warping toward straight lines and translational motion helped ~1 dB; warping away from them (polar strip) cost 82 % bytes. Block codecs want shape-preserving, straight-line motion. Note also that the intra gain exceeds the inter gain, so a still-image test would overstate a geometry effect relative to video.

Ahmmed, Hannuksela & Gabbouj 2016 - fully deformable motion on fisheye: at most 6.5 %

A. Ahmmed, M. M. Hannuksela, M. Gabbouj, "Fisheye video coding using elastic motion compensated reference frames," IEEE ICIP 2016, pp. 2027-2031, doi:10.1109/ICIP.2016.7532714. Link: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7532714/ Verified: abstract (IEEE record and Tampere research portal). - What they did: elastic-motion warped prediction of the previous frame inserted as an extra reference picture in HM (low-delay P), no low-level tool changes; "savings in bit rate of up to 6.54% ... over standalone HEVC" (LectureB 1080x1080). - For round selfies: an upper bound on what sophisticated non-translational motion buys on round wide-angle content, even with a general deformable model: single digits, far below the +37 % (elliptical) / +82 % (polar) penalties of re-gridding.

Jin, Saxena & Budagavi 2015 - Samsung: lens-aware warped motion inside HEVC

G. Jin, A. Saxena, M. Budagavi, "Motion estimation and compensation for fisheye warped video," IEEE ICIP 2015, pp. 2751-2755, doi:10.1109/ICIP.2015.7351303. Link: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIP.2015.7351303 Verified: abstract via IEEE record; no numbers in abstract, full text paywalled. - What they did: warping motion compensation modelling the fisheye distortion; lens parameters sent once per sequence, per-block warping derived implicitly on both sides, "no block-level warping parameters are transmitted". - For round selfies: again geometry knowledge is injected into the motion model and the pixel grid is left alone. No bitrate figure is public, so treat it as design precedent only.

Vishwanath, Rose, He & Ye 2018 - the one big geometry-aware gain (~11.5 %) needs a badly warped projection

B. Vishwanath, K. Rose, Y. He, Y. Ye, "Rotational Motion Compensated Prediction in HEVC Based Omnidirectional Video Coding," Picture Coding Symposium 2018, pp. 323-327, doi:10.1109/PCS.2018.8456296. Link: https://scl.ece.ucsb.edu/sites/default/files/publications/08456296_0.pdf Verified: full text read (Table I). - What they did: block motion as rotation along geodesics on the sphere inside HM-16.15, ERP input, WS-PSNR, random access. Y BD-rate vs HEVC: bicyclist -12.4 %, chair -15.9 %, glacier -17.9 %, brancastle -6.3 %, driving-in-country -11.4 %, kiteflite -5.4 %; average -11.5 %. - For round selfies: shows the size of the prize when a projection really breaks translational motion: ~10 %. A selfie disc is a plain perspective crop whose motion is already translational; re-projecting it into polar creates the very warping this paper had to fight.

360-degree projection formats - uniform sampling wins

I. Hussain, O.-J. Kwon, S. Choi, "Evaluating the Coding Performance of 360 Image Projection Formats Using Objective Quality Metrics," Symmetry 13(1):80, 2021, doi:10.3390/sym13010080. F. Duanmu, Y. He, X. Xiu, P. Hanhart, Y. Ye, Y. Wang, "Hybrid Cubemap Projection Format for 360-Degree Video Coding," IEEE Data Compression Conference 2018, p. 404 (poster abstract), doi:10.1109/DCC.2018.00075. J.-L. Lin et al., "Efficient Projection and Coding Tools for 360 Video," IEEE JETCAS 9(1):84-97, 2019, doi:10.1109/JETCAS.2019.2899660. Links: https://doi.org/10.3390/sym13010080 ; https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8416621/ ; https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8643362/ Verified: Hussain abstract (MDPI record); Duanmu/Hanhart author list and venue (IEEE record), 11.51 % / 8.0 % / 0.54 % vs ERP / CMP / ACP from the NYU Video Lab project page, not the paper body; Lin abstract (35.5 % Y BD-rate vs padded ERP in 360Lib-5.0/HM-16.16, together with 360-specific coding tools). - What they did: converted 360 content into many projection formats, coded with JPEG/JPEG 2000 (Hussain) or HM+360Lib (Duanmu, Lin) and compared end-to-end quality. The most uniformly sampled formats (hybrid equi-angular cubemap, equatorial cylindrical) code best. - For round selfies: a polar strip of a disc is the ERP problem in miniature (massively oversampled centre, a seam). Our equal-area-bands variant (1.76x) is the analogue of EAC/HEC and helped versus plain polar (1.82x), but cannot beat the square, which is already uniformly sampled.

AV1 warped motion - rotation/zoom are already in the codec, worth ~1 % on average

S. Parker, Y. Chen, D. Barker, P. de Rivaz, D. Mukherjee, "Global and locally adaptive warped motion compensation in video compression," IEEE ICIP 2017, pp. 275-279, doi:10.1109/ICIP.2017.8296286. J. Han et al., "A Technical Overview of AV1," Proc. IEEE 109(9):1435-1462, 2021; arXiv:2008.06091. Visionular, "Performance analysis of AV1 encoding tools: libaom case study." Links: https://doi.org/10.1109/ICIP.2017.8296286 ; https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06091 ; https://visionular.ai/performance-analysis-of-av1-encoding-tools-libaom-case-study/ Verified: Parker abstract (IEEE/Semantic Scholar record: global motion mode + locally adaptive warped motion, affine/homographic); Han abstract (">30% reduction in bit-rate compared to ... VP9", "considerations for hardware feasibility"); Visionular page read. - What they did: AV1's frame-level global motion (affine/homographic) and block-level warped motion (affine derived from neighbouring MVs, no extra side info). Visionular measured tool-off deltas in libaom at cpu-used 3: warped motion 1.07 % BD-rate for ~2 % CPU; ~4 % on a rotating Netflix clip, ~8 % on "blue_sky"; global-motion pre-analysis was disabled at that preset. - For round selfies: head tilt and leaning in (rotation, zoom) are already modelled inside AV1 (and VVC affine) at zero cost to the pixel grid, worth ~1 % on ordinary content and up to ~8 % on genuinely rotating clips. Sets expectations for the pending AV1 test: the square should stay smallest.

Affine motion in HEVC/VVC - 1-2 % average, 10-20 % only on rotation/zoom-heavy clips

L. Li, H. Li, D. Liu, Z. Li, H. Yang, S. Lin, H. Chen, F. Wu, "An Efficient Four-Parameter Affine Motion Model for Video Coding," IEEE TCSVT 28(8):1934-1948, 2018; arXiv:1702.06297 (2017). K. Zhang, Y.-W. Chen, L. Zhang, W.-J. Chien, M. Karczewicz, "An Improved Framework of Affine Motion Compensation in Video Coding," IEEE TIP 28(3):1456-1469, 2019, doi:10.1109/TIP.2018.2877355. H. Yang, H. Chen, J. Chen, S. Esenlik, S. Sethuraman, X. Xiu, E. Alshina, J. Luo, "Subblock-Based Motion Derivation and Inter Prediction Refinement in the VVC Standard," IEEE TCSVT 31(10):3862-3877, 2021, doi:10.1109/TCSVT.2021.3100744. Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06297 ; https://doi.org/10.1109/TIP.2018.2877355 ; https://doi.org/10.1109/TCSVT.2021.3100744 Verified: all three abstracts. - What they did: Li: 4-parameter affine (translation + rotation + uniform zoom) in HEVC, "11.1% and 19.3% bits saving for random access and low delay" on sequences with rich rotation/zoom. Zhang: adaptive 4/6-parameter models and unified merge (parts adopted in VVC), "1.67% BD rate savings in average for the random access configuration" under JVET CTC. Yang: the five VVC sub-block tools together give "5.7% bitrate reduction on average", "up to 13.4%" on large/complex-motion sequences. - For round selfies: standards bodies quantified exactly the motions a polar grid was meant to make cheap. On average content they are worth 1-2 %; only rotation/zoom-heavy clips reach 10-20 %. Hand-held selfies are dominated by small translations and jitter, so a coordinate change cannot recover more than a few percent even in theory.

Global motion compensation - big gains only for a moving camera over a static scene

T. Wiegand, E. Steinbach, B. Girod, "Affine multipicture motion-compensated prediction," IEEE TCSVT 15(2):197-209, 2005, doi:10.1109/TCSVT.2004.841690. F. Dufaux, J. Konrad, "Efficient, robust, and fast global motion estimation for video coding," IEEE TIP 9(3):497-501, 2000, doi:10.1109/83.826785. Links: https://web.stanford.edu/~bgirod/pdfs/WiegandCSVT2005.pdf ; https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/826785/ Verified: Wiegand full text (abstract); Dufaux abstract via IEEE record. - What they did: Wiegand: several affine parameter sets per frame, whole reference pictures warped into a long-term multipicture buffer, ordinary block translation on top (H.263 TMN-10): "average bit-rate savings of 15%" with 20 warped references, 24 % with 20 warped + 10 decoded references over eight sequences, PSNR gains 0.8-3 dB. Dufaux & Konrad: hierarchical, robust estimator from translation up to an 8-parameter perspective model for MPEG-4-style global motion compensation. - For round selfies: GMC pays for camera pan/zoom over static scenes. A circle message is the opposite: near-static background, moving foreground, small hand jitter. The design lesson carried into AV1's global-motion mode is the same, and its measured value on ordinary content is ~1 %.

Ng, Po, Cheung & Wong 2010 - explicit rotation search: angles are small in talking-head video

K.-H. Ng, L.-M. Po, K.-W. Cheung, K.-M. Wong, "Block-Matching Translational and Rotational Motion Compensated Prediction Using Interpolated Reference Frame," EURASIP J. Advances in Signal Processing 2010:385631, doi:10.1155/2010/385631. Link: https://asp-eurasipjournals.springeropen.com/articles/10.1155/2010/385631 Verified: abstract and body text (open access). - What they did: rotated reference blocks built by special sub-sampling of the interpolated reference frame, block matching over translation x angle. Body text: "up to 37% of the blocks can be better predicted with rotational MCP. The angle of rotation is usually small, especially for static video sequences like Akiyo"; Akiyo was the one sequence where rotational MCP at 1/8-pel did not beat translational MCP at 1/16-pel. - For round selfies: direct evidence that per-frame rotation in talking-head content is tiny. Our synthetic clips say the same from the other side: polar wins only at 3 deg/frame spin (-14 %) and loses at 1 deg/frame (+19 %).

Fourier-Mellin / log-polar as an estimator, not a coding domain

B. S. Reddy, B. N. Chatterji, "An FFT-based technique for translation, rotation, and scale-invariant image registration," IEEE TIP 5(8):1266-1271, 1996, doi:10.1109/83.506761. H. T. Ho, R. Goecke, "Optical flow estimation using Fourier Mellin Transform," IEEE CVPR 2008, pp. 1-8, doi:10.1109/CVPR.2008.4587553. Links: https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/506761/ ; https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/4587553/ Verified: both abstracts via IEEE/ANU records. - What they did: Reddy & Chatterji: phase correlation on the log-polar-resampled Fourier magnitude recovers rotation and scale, then phase correlation recovers translation ("excellent robustness against random noise"). Ho & Goecke: applied per patch on a grid to estimate a full similarity-transform optical flow with sub-pixel accuracy. - For round selfies: this is the legitimate home of "polar maths" in video: as a motion estimator. A defensible way to keep the polar idea alive is a Fourier-Mellin rotation/zoom estimator feeding a standard codec's affine/warped mode (or a stabiliser), with pixels left on the square grid.

Iris biometrics - the only field that stored a disc "unwrapped" in polar form, and moved back

S. Matschitsch, M. Tschinder, A. Uhl, "Comparison of Compression Algorithms' Impact on Iris Recognition Accuracy," ICB 2007, LNCS 4642, pp. 232-241, doi:10.1007/978-3-540-74549-5_25. J. Daugman, C. Downing, "Effect of severe image compression on iris recognition performance," IEEE TIFS 3(1):52-61, 2008 (Cambridge TR UCAM-CL-TR-685, 2007). P. Grother, "Quantitative Standardization of Iris Image Formats," BIOSIG 2009 (NIST IREX I). Links: https://www.cosy.sbg.ac.at/~uhl/iris07_final.pdf ; https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/techreports/UCAM-CL-TR-685.pdf ; https://www.nist.gov/itl/iad/btg/irex-i Verified: Matschitsch full text ("polar images ... 2kB vs. 25-30kB for rectilinear images"); Daugman full report; IREX summary via NIST documents and Grother's paper. - What they did: compared JPEG / JPEG 2000 / SPIHT etc. on rectilinear vs polar (unwrapped) iris images for recognition accuracy; Daugman & Downing pushed ROI-isolated rectilinear 320x320 images to 2,000 bytes ("net factor of 150") with only "2% to 3% of the bits in the IrisCode" changed. NIST IREX I found the cropped (K3) and cropped+masked (K7) rectilinear formats interoperable and the unsegmented polar (K16) format underperforming; the polar formats were dropped from the ISO/IEC 19794-6:2011 revision. - For round selfies: the closest literal "disc coded in polar coordinates" precedent is a still-image, single-purpose case where polar unwrapping was a cropping convenience, not a compression advantage, and the standard went back to rectilinear. No video-coding paper was found that codes a circular video in polar coordinates and reports an RD win.

Lens 2. Still-image coding in polar / log-polar / disc-native bases

Traver & Bernardino 2010 - thirty years of log-polar imaging, no rate-distortion result

V. J. Traver, A. Bernardino, "A review of log-polar imaging for visual perception in robotics," Robotics and Autonomous Systems 58(4):378-398, 2010. Link: https://vislab.isr.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/10-ras-traver.pdf Verified: full text read (229 references). - What they did: survey of log-polar (retina-like) imaging in tracking, attention, egomotion and 3D perception, including the origin singularity, over-sampling of the centre and lost invariances. Conclusion: "While linearities and translation invariance are certainly lost in log-polar images ... real-time active target tracking and time-to-impact estimation are possibly the tasks that log-polar imaging is clearly best suited to." No bit-rate numbers anywhere; compression appears only via the Weiman citation. - For round selfies: independent confirmation of the two mechanisms the video test found (oversampled centre = redundant pixels; sideways motion stops being a translation). The review's remedies (blind central disc, active camera keeping the target centred) are not available for a selfie circle.

Weiman 1990 / US 5,103,306 - see Lens 1

Same entry as above; for the still-image question the relevant point is that the 25:1 log-polar stage is foveation (fewer pixels toward the rim), so there is no equal-quality still-image result to build on.

Berton, Sandini & Metta 2006 - real log-polar silicon: fewer pixels, a seam at the fovea, no market

F. Berton, G. Sandini, G. Metta, "Anthropomorphic Visual Sensors," Encyclopedia of Sensors (eds. ISO/IEC 19794-6:2005 - the one standardised polar still-image format

Daugman & Downing 2008 - the inventor of iris recognition rejects polar images as an interchange format

J. Daugman, C. Downing, "Effect of severe image compression on iris recognition performance," IEEE TIFS 3(1):52-61, 2008; TR UCAM-CL-TR-685, 2007. Rakshit & Monro 2007 - polar iris strips compress well because the consumer is a matcher

Lens 3. Why the pipeline is rectangular, and where non-rectangular thinking actually shipped

Bayer 1976 / Wikipedia - colour capture starts on a square grid

B. E. Bayer, US patent 3,971,065 (1976); Wikipedia, "Bayer filter". Link: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_filter Verified: Wikipedia lead read. - What it says: "A Bayer filter mosaic is a color filter array (CFA) for arranging RGB color filters on a square grid of photosensors", "half green, one quarter red and one quarter blue"; CMOS sensors read this grid row by row. - For round selfies: the pixels the phone hands you are already Cartesian and demosaiced on a square lattice; a polar representation is a second resampling on top and can only add error, never recover rim detail that was never sampled radially.

Block codecs are square-block machines (H.264 / HEVC / AV1)

J. Han et al., "A Technical Overview of AV1," Proc. IEEE 109(9):1435-1462, 2021; arXiv:2008.06091. Wikipedia: "Advanced Video Coding", "High Efficiency Video Coding", "AV1". Links: https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06091 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AV1 Verified: Han abstract; Wikipedia sentences read. - What they say: H.264 "variable block-size motion compensation (VBSMC) with block sizes as large as 16x16 and as small as 4x4" (finalised May 2003); "HEVC replaces 16x16 pixel macroblocks ... with coding tree units (CTUs) which can use larger block structures of up to 64x64 samples"; AV1 "Superblocks are square-shaped and can either be of size 128x128 or 64x64 pixels"; AV1 "achieves more than 30% reduction in bit-rate compared to its predecessor VP9" and was designed "with considerations for hardware feasibility". Wikipedia also cites Netflix 2017 (AV1 "about 25% more efficient than VP9") and Facebook 2018 (34 %, 46.2 %, 50.3 % vs libvpx-vp9, x264 High, x264 Main). - For round selfies: a 384-px circle is 24x24 macroblocks or 3x3 AV1 superblocks; every tool (prediction, transforms, motion vectors, entropy contexts) assumes axis-aligned squares, so a polar canvas gets no help and its seam/rim lands on block edges as new content. Bigger AV1 blocks make flat white corners even cheaper in the square, so the gap is unlikely to close (pending batch3).

Phone hardware encoders: ROI only as rectangles / block QP offsets (Android, V4L2)

Android MediaCodec.PARAMETER_KEY_QP_OFFSET_RECTS / _MAP and NDK feature "Roi" (flagged API android.media.codec.region_of_interest); AOSP "OEM guidance for RoI implementation"; D. Guthyappa Madivalara (Qualcomm), "[RFC PATCH 1/3] media: uapi: Introduce new control for video encoder ROI," linux-media, Jan 2026 (reply by N. Dufresne quoting it). Daala PVQ - "polar" that shipped in a codec lives in coefficient space

J.-M. Valin, T. B. Terriberry, "Perceptual Vector Quantization for Video Coding," Proc. The profitable place for polar maths is inside the quantiser or a perceptual loss, not in warping pixels.

VVC Geometric Partitioning Mode - a heavily engineered non-rectangular tool is worth ~1 %

Implemented as a GPU rasteriser feature on a rectangular render target. - For round selfies: foveation gets 30-50 % by rendering fewer pixels in a huge periphery (~100 deg FOV). A 384-px selfie circle has almost no periphery, so expect single-digit to low-double-digit percent at best, via QP/resolution hints, not coordinate changes.

Gaze-steered QP in ordinary H.264 - up to 63 % at JND, but only with live gaze

O. Wiedemann, V. Hosu, H. Lin, D. Saupe, "Foveated Video Coding for Real-Time Streaming Applications," QoMEX 2020, doi:10.1109/QoMEX48832.2020.9123080. G. Illahi, M. Siekkinen, E. Masala, "Foveated Video Streaming for Cloud Gaming," IEEE MMSP 2017; arXiv:1706.04804. Links: https://kops.uni-konstanz.de/entities/publication/9cff8858-f7fe-40f0-9220-7c387f0670ba ; https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04804 Verified: both abstracts. - What they did: eye tracker feeds the H.264 encoder's spatial QP allocation in real time; "average bitrate savings of 63.24% at the JND in comparison to the unfoveated baseline" (Wiedemann). Illahi: foveated cloud-gaming streaming "can reduce bandwidth requirements by even more than 50% depending on parametrization". - For round selfies: upper bound for spatially non-uniform quality on a square grid, achieved only with live gaze and a large field of view. A stored circle has no gaze feedback and the salient region is ~the whole disc; a static face-ROI/rim-deprioritisation is the applicable subset, and the metric must be perceptual (JND), not disc PSNR, or the saving will look like a loss.

Hsiao et al. 2022 - non-uniform allocation is a latency problem, not a geometry problem

L. Hsiao, B. Krajancich, P. Levis, G. Wetzstein, K. Winstein, "Towards Retina-Quality VR Video Streaming: 15ms Could Save You 80% of Your Bandwidth," ACM SIGCOMM CCR 52(1):10-19, 2022; arXiv:2108.12720. Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.12720 Verified: abstract and CCR record. - What they did: client/server VR streaming with sub-15 ms motion-to-photon latency and gaze-contingent compression; "a client and streaming server system with sub-15ms latency can achieve 5x better compression than traditional techniques while also using simpler software algorithms". - For round selfies: a stored circle message has no feedback loop, so its achievable non-uniform gain is whatever a static prior (face centred, rim less important, corners invisible) can give. Frames "where do we profit from polar?" as "what do we know a priori about where the viewer looks?".

Qualcomm 2005 patent - face/ROI bit allocation for video telephony is old industry practice

Qualcomm Inc., "Region-of-interest coding in video telephony using RHO domain bit allocation," US 8,768,084 B2, priority 1 Mar 2005, granted 1 Jul 2014. Related: libvpx VP9E_SET_ROI_MAP ("Extend Region of Interest functionality to VP9", libvpx 1.8.0, Jan 2019). Links: https://patents.google.com/patent/US8768084B2/en ; https://chromium.googlesource.com/webm/libvpx/+/v1.8.0 Verified: patent abstract; libvpx changelog. - What it says: "adaptive skipping of a non-ROI area of a video frame to conserve encoding bits for allocation to the ROI", "allocation of bits to the ROI using weighted bit allocation models at the macroblock (MB) level within the rho domain", plus an ROI-weighted quality metric. No bitrate percentages. No public engineering documentation was found that Zoom / Meet / Teams apply face ROI in production. - For round selfies: the industry answer to "the face matters more than the background" is macroblock-level ROI on the square grid, decided by a face detector, not a change of coordinates. For circle messages: lower QP on the face, higher on the rim ring, maximum QP or skip on the masked corners, all H.264-compatible.

Shirley & Chiu 1997 - the concentric map was designed for sampling, not compression

P. Shirley, K. Chiu, "A Low Distortion Map Between Disk and Square," Journal of Graphics Tools 2(3):45-52, 1997, doi:10.1080/10867651.1997.10487479. Links: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/10867651.1997.10487479 ; code https://github.com/erich666/jgt-code (Volume_02/Number_3/Shirley1997) Verified: abstract via publisher/ACM record; public-domain code confirmed. - What they did: a map between squares and discs "that associates concentric squares with concentric circles ... preserves adjacency and fractional area", with branch-minimal robust code and a hemisphere extension. Area is preserved; angles are distorted most along the square's diagonals. - For round selfies: explains why the Shirley-Chiu variant (1.62x) sat between elliptical (1.37x) and polar (1.8-2.1x): it keeps neighbourhoods but bends straight lines along the diagonals, and its corners map onto the rim where the loss concentrates. A motion-compensated block codec punishes shear most, which is why the near-affine elliptical stretch lost the least.

Bottom line across all three lenses

  1. No paper codes a circular video in polar coordinates with a standard codec and reports an equal-quality bitrate win. Every "polar compresses" number in the literature (Weiman 25:1, Sandini sensors, Wang-Bovik, Varjo) comes from foveation, i.e. from giving the rim less quality.
  2. Where geometry-aware ideas did pay (fisheye ~1 dB, 360-degree rotation ~11 %, AV1/VVC affine 1-6 %), they were put into the motion model or the quantiser and the pixel grid was left alone; the only large gains needed a projection far more warped than a selfie crop.
  3. The cheapest experiments that keep the square grid and are backed by prior art: (a) x264 addroi / AV1 delta-q with the disc as ROI; (b) an intra-only (still-image) split of the 82-point penalty; (c) a Fourier-Mellin rotation/zoom estimator as a front-end, if the polar idea is to be kept alive at all.
